## Defect 1 — the console cannot be closed on touch (ROOT CAUSE FOUND)

The game's world-touch handler (game3.js, document `touchstart`, `{passive:false}`) claims every touch that does NOT land inside the `TOUCH_UI` selector list, calling `e.preventDefault()` and treating the touch as a world look-drag / tap (a quick tap fires strike/chisel on release). `TOUCH_UI` = `#touchui, #bottombar, #bottomright, #readout, #board, #realms, #subspace, #intro-viewer, #login-screen`.

**`#nfconsole` is not in that list.** The console panel is a top-level overlay (page3.html line 748). Consequences on a phone, all code-provable:
- Every tap on the console panel is `preventDefault()`ed at touchstart, which suppresses the compatibility `click` — so `#nfconsole-close`'s only handler (`click` → `nfConsoleToggle(false)`, game3.js:6271) **never fires**. The close button is dead on touch.
- Worse, taps on the panel are interpreted as world taps: a quick tap on the panel makes the fox strike/chisel, drags look around. The panel is a trap that punishes trying to use it.
- The panel's own `click` handler (focus-the-input) is equally dead, and the input can only be focused by the open-time programmatic `focus()` — matching Will's screenshot: he got text INTO the input ("1.1.1") via the auto-focus on open, but had no working way out.
- The only other close paths are Backquote/Escape keys (no such keys on a phone soft keyboard in reach) and `#btn-console`'s `click` toggle — that button lives in `#bottomright` (inside TOUCH_UI, so its click does fire), but with the console open + soft keyboard up it sits under the keyboard/panel area, and nothing tells the player it's the exit.
- **A running probe cannot be cancelled at all**: `nfConsoleRun` sets `nfCon.busy` and `nfWalkProbe` walks the fox on the scheme's forward key for up to 12 s per leg (`runall` = 3 legs ≈ up to 36 s). There is no cancellation flag anywhere; closing the console (had it worked) would not stop the walk — the fox keeps auto-walking after the pane closes. "Couldn't get back to anything useful" is literal.

FIX (planned): add `#nfconsole` (and `#speedrun`, same bug class — see below) to TOUCH_UI; give the console touch-first controls in the game's own `tBtn` idiom (touchstart handlers, not click-only): close button closes on touchstart, `#btn-console` toggles on touchstart; add a stop button in the console row, visible while a probe runs; wire a cancellation flag through `nfWalkProbe`/`nfRunAll` — closing the console or tapping stop cancels the running probe and releases the forward key.

**Same bug class, found while tracing**: `#speedrun` (page3.html line 756) is also a top-level overlay missing from TOUCH_UI — its buttons (arm/close/etc.) are click-only and equally dead on touch. Same one-line exemption fixes it; included in scope as the same defect.

## Defect 2 — rift/tune entry from touch (ROOT CAUSE FOUND, narrower than feared)

The note pads (`.notepad`, built into `#instrument` inside `#bottombar`) DO live inside a TOUCH_UI subtree, so their taps are not preventDefaulted, and their `click` handler calls the real `playNote` — the same function the physical keys call. So pads are not swallowed by the console's isolation (which is keyboard-only: capture-phase keydown/keyup). What actually stranded Will: with the console open and its input auto-focused, the soft keyboard covers the bottom of the screen where `#bottombar` sits, and the console could not be closed (Defect 1) — the pads were unreachable behind the keyboard, and number keys typed into the console input instead of playing notes (his "1.1.1"). The fix for Defect 1 (a closable console) removes the trap; additionally the pads get `touchstart` handlers in the tBtn idiom so a pad tap registers the note directly on touch (no dependence on click synthesis), pinned by a unit check + the probe's three-note tune leg (pads 1-4-9 must open the same rift the keys open).

## Defect 3 — phone layout (FINDINGS)

All overlays are `position:fixed; z-index:5` (`.overlay`); `#nfconsole` is `left:14px; top:110px; width:min(620px,94vw)`, z-11. On a portrait phone (~360-400 CSS px wide):
- `#topleft` (left, flex, ~190px), `#topcenter` (centered), `#topright` (right, content-width incl. the long quest line) all sit at top:14px and mutually overlap — visible in Will's screenshot (objective/quest panels stacked over the portrait panel).
- `#nfconsole` at top:110px overlaps `#topright`'s lower lines and covers the world's center; its output pane is max-height 38vh.
- `#help` (extras) is an unconstrained-width text wall at bottom-left that wraps across the whole lower screen, visually burying the note pads and `#bottomright`'s button row (screenshot shows exactly this); `#bottomright`'s 10-button row is wider than a phone screen and wraps over the pads.
FIX (planned, minimal, small-screen media query only — desktop untouched): below 680px, (a) `#nfconsole` becomes left:6px/right:6px/width:auto/top:6px with z-index above the top panels and a shorter output pane (30vh) — a modal working surface while open, now reliably closable; (b) `#topright` drops below `#topleft` (top offset) so the three top panels stack instead of colliding; (c) `#help` gets max-height + scroll and side insets so it stops burying the pads; (d) `#bottomright` wraps to the right edge with smaller gaps, clear of `#instrument`.

Refusal: honest toast, and the refusal path returns BEFORE any flip, record, or wooze — no partial state.

## Item 4b — INVERTED PLAYABILITY (ruling 2026-11-26 20:27 CST: "the world still needs to still be playable after the inversion")

The once-per-day rule is NOT loosened and free reversal is NOT added (Will, 20:27). The requirement: after an inversion the realm stays traversable end-to-end while inverted. Code investigation, point by point:

**What already works (verified in code):**
- The physics is genuinely two-sided: `playerBox()` flips the body span (feet are the box TOP when inverted), the Y-clamp has an inverted branch, the one-block step assist steps "toward away-from-ground, so it works on a ceiling too", `doJump` jumps along `gravDir`, strafe sign follows gravity, and when down changes under the fox the body span is preserved (`pos.y -= PHEIGHT * g`) so a flip never ratchets the fox through a floor.
- The walking surface exists: `baseBlockAt` lays the riftstone vault (blocks at CEIL_Y=46 and 47) over every column of a realm whose gravity is inverted — the inverted fox falls up and lands on the vault's underside at feet y=46 and walks it. Collision is functional (`getBlockWorld` → cached chunk data, falling back to live `baseBlockAt`), and `chunkSignature` INCLUDES the realm's gravity, so chunks generated before a flip are detected stale and regenerated by `chunkMaintenance` as the fox approaches — the vault assembles ahead of the walker. A flip also force-regenerates the 3×3 chunks around the fox and dirties the whole current scroll.
- Fall-home works both ways: leaving the world (`pos.y < -12 || pos.y > WORLD_H + 14`) fires BEAT-fall, hurts, and respawns at Arrival — and respawn-while-inverted is survivable: the fox lands at Arrival's ground, falls up to the vault above Arrival (Arrival's ward covers the ground), and plays on. No loop: the Arrival scroll's chunks carry the vault via the same signature self-heal.
- Inside the Unexpected Hall, inverted traversal mostly works: the hall interior (floor top y=5, ceiling block y=11, 6 open rows) is CARVE-open except full-height pillars you walk around on the ceiling exactly as on the floor, and the exit is natural — rise through the entrance shaft (its spiral footholds are for normal-gravity climbing; inverted, the open shaft center is the way up and out).

**What blocks it (defects to fix):**
- **IP-1 PORTALS ARE DEAD WHILE INVERTED.** `riftEnd` anchors every ring to the GROUND (`feetY = heightAt(...)`), and `riftStep`'s crossing gate is `|dy| < 2.4` against that ground feetY, with `dy` computed from the fox's foot plane. An inverted fox stands at y≈46, ~25+ blocks above any ground ring: the tune still opens a rift (they can watch it stand below, unreachable), but crossing can never fire. Realm travel — the game's core verb — is gone for the rest of the day in that realm. FIX: `riftEnd` anchors an end to the surface its realm's gravity presses you to: ground normally; when the realm is inverted, `feetY = CEIL_Y - 1` (so the fox's foot plane, feet at CEIL_Y, reads dy ≈ 0 exactly as the ground case does) and the ring mesh hangs below the vault instead of standing on the ground. Landings through a link (`doCross` viaLink, `crossThroughRift` same-realm) place the fox on the destination's gravity surface (vault plane when inverted) instead of always at ground height.
- **IP-2 THE DEEP STONE IS UNREACHABLE FROM THE HALL CEILING — OFF BY THE BODY SPAN.** The quest check (`sub.checkQuest(bx, floor(pos.y), bz)`, world.js) and the speedrun finish (game3.js, `deepStoneNear` with the same `by`) sample the fox's block row at `pos.y` — the FEET row under normal gravity. Inverted, the feet are the body's top: ceiling-walking the hall, `floor(pos.y)` = 10–11, and the stone's tolerance is |by − 6| ≤ 3 (by ≤ 9). The fox can stand on the ceiling directly over the stone and never complete the quest. FIX: judge reach against the body's span — near if either end of the body (feet row or the far end, `pos.y + gravDir * PHEIGHT`) is within tolerance. Both call sites; `checkQuest`/`deepStoneNear` signatures unchanged.
- **IP-3 THE ENTRANCE SHAFT PUNCHES A DEATH-HOLE THROUGH THE VAULT.** In the shaft columns (3×3 at 44–46, 30–32), `dungeonBlockAt` returns CARVE for every y ≥ surface height — including y = 46/47 — so under inverted gravity the vault is missing exactly over the shaft: an inverted fox walking the vault over the barrow hill steps onto nothing and falls UP into open sky until the fall-home bound (hurt + respawn). A one-way hole in the only road. FIX: in `baseBlockAt`, the vault rule wins over the shaft carve at vault heights — when the realm is inverted, y ∈ {CEIL_Y, CEIL_Y+1} is VAULT in every column, shaft included. Under normal gravity nothing changes (no vault exists). The shaft still exits fine inverted: rising inside it, the fox slides sideways out from under the cap into open air above the surface and up to the vault beside the shaft.
- **Boundary, stated plainly (not a defect):** while a realm is inverted, its GROUND is out of reach — gravity admits no descent, so the barrow's shaft mouth (a ground door) cannot be entered from the vault side until the realm turns again. What the fixes guarantee: the inverted world itself is fully playable — vault traversal end-to-end, portal crossings out (and home again by a fresh tune, landing on the vault), the Hall completable and exitable when entered/flipped inside, fall-home and respawn recovering to the vault — and nothing about the player is trapped or soft-locked by having flipped. One pre-existing exception, found by the gate probe and recorded in the Gates section: the SAME door does not stand for the walk back after a realm cross (true at ground level too, and in demo26) — the return is a fresh tune, not the standing door, until that defect gets its own fix.

Proof plan: unit pins for the pure parts (vault-over-shaft and vault presence in `baseBlockAt` under both gravities; the ledger pins already exist and stay untouched) + source pins for the rift anchoring, plus a deterministic Jenkins probe leg (in probe-touch.mjs): flip in the meadow → land on the vault → walk 70+ blocks across a scroll border, passing over the shaft without falling out → place into the Hall (setup placement, traversal itself on real keys/physics) → ceiling-walk to the stone's column → quest completes → rise out the shaft to the vault → play the bridge tune on the vault → walk through the ring → realm changes; cross back → land on the vault.
