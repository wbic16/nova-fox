## 1. The offer (RULED)

- **Free tier — always free.** Permanent, not a trial, not a timed
  demo. It is the front door: the whole taught world (Nova Prime +
  Ashfall Haven), the guided opening, the quest, the daily pocket.
- **Paid tier — $5, one time, unlocks the game for life.** Sold on
  Gumroad. Its advantages must be **obvious** — visible, tangible,
  understandable before buying.
- **The headline paid advantage (RULED): generator status.** A paid
  player is a token generator: their play spews free-tier tokens
  into the world and earns them **credits** while they do it. A free
  player only drips.

## 2. The economy (RULED shape, PROPOSAL numbers)

### 2.1 Two currencies

- **Tokens** — the world's working currency. Player-facing name:
  PROPOSAL "flux" (the fiction already uses the word: the dark bulk
  "pressing in as flux" — DESIGN.md storyline). Alternative: keep
  "tokens". Will picks.
- **Credits** — the premium currency. Player-facing name: PROPOSAL
  keep "credits" (plain English, every player knows the word).
  Credits have exactly two sinks (RULED): **power-ups** and
  **gifting the game**.

### 2.2 Generation

- **Free tier (RULED):** tokens generate **slowly** — a drip.
  PROPOSAL: the drip is tied to active play (exploring, shaping,
  playing tunes, clearing pockets), not to an idle open tab; draft
  rate one token per few minutes of active play, exact number
  Will's.
- **Paid tier (RULED):** the player **is** a generator. Their play
  spews tokens — draft mechanics (PROPOSAL): verbs that make the
  world richer generate: opening a new place, shaping, clearing a
  pocket, finishing the daily, playing a tune that opens a rift.
  While spewing, the generator earns credits at a draft ratio of
  **1 credit per 10 tokens spewed** (illustrative).
- **Caps (PROPOSAL, sanity):** a per-hour credit cap and a per-day
  spew cap, with soft diminishing returns past them — generation
  rewards play, it must not reward leaving the game running or
  grinding one verb in a corner.

### 2.3 Where the spewed tokens land (PROPOSAL — Will picks)

1. **The pool:** spewed tokens raise a shared pool that lifts every
   free player's drip rate. Nobody sees who gave; everybody feels
   it. (The cooperative shape: paid play literally feeds free play.)
2. **Nearby:** spewed tokens land on free players in the same realm
   — visible generosity, names attached, social glue.
3. **Split (draft recommendation):** half to nearby free players
   (seen, thanked), half to the pool (the tide that lifts the drip).

### 2.4 What tokens are spent on (PROPOSAL — anchored to what exists)

Tokens are the small-change of the world; draft sinks, all from
verbs the game already has:

- **Riding a rift to a far pocket** beyond the chords you know —
  the jump is a rift taken deliberately; a long ride costs tokens.
- **Posting your marks to the shared world** — inscriptions are
  forever; a small token cost is also the honest answer to the
  shared gateway's rate cap (120 posts/min across everyone).
- **Entering the daily challenge pocket** — a token stake to enter;
  clearing it pays tokens back with a prize chance (interacts with
  §6 D6/D7, the unruled prize shape).
- **Borrowing the grand brushes for a session** — if Will rules the
  big brushes are tools-not-power-ups (see §3 note), tokens rent
  what credits would buy outright.

PROPOSAL exclusions: tokens never gate the guide, the quest, the
taught chords, or the flip — the front door stays a front door.

### 2.5 Credits and power-ups (RULED: every power-up needs credits)

The audit's power-up inventory (AUDIT §5) is the pricing draft's
starting set: the Lance, the lamp, the Bright Charm, the grand
brushes, chisel degrees, the spectral flute, chip-tune melodies,
and future items. Draft price shape (PROPOSAL, illustrative):
small advantages 5–15 credits (melodies, flute), working advantages
20–40 (grand brushes, Bright Charm), the gift at 100 (§2.6).

Two honest complications, put to Will in §6 rather than smoothed:

- The **Lance is quest-critical** — it is the only weapon, and the
  free tier's quest cannot be finished without it. If every
  power-up needs credits and free players can hold none, the free
  quest breaks. Options: the Lance is a *tool of the guide*, given
  in the opening (not classed a power-up); or the guide's gift
  includes the credits for exactly one Lance. Will rules.
- Whether the lamp is a power-up or a sense (like sight) — same
  shape of question, smaller stakes.

### 2.6 Credits gift the game (RULED) — the growth loop

**Credits can buy the game for a friend**: a generator spends
credits (draft price **100 credits**, set against the $5 cash
price — PROPOSAL) and a friend gets the full paid tier, for life.

The loop this closes: paid players generate tokens for the free
tier and earn credits → credits gift paid unlocks → gifted friends
become generators themselves → more spew, more credits, more
gifts. **Growth is endogenous, funded by play** — nobody has to be
sold twice, and the free tier is fed by the paid tier's activity,
not by a marketing budget.

Draft mechanics (PROPOSAL):

- **One key system, two issuers.** Gumroad purchases and credit
  gifts produce the same kind of unlock key, redeemed the same way
  at the start screen — so support, wording, and code have one
  path, not two.
- **Delivery:** the giver names the friend's player name in-game,
  or takes a gift key to hand over outside the game (a code in a
  message works everywhere). Both drafted; Will picks.
- **Gifted accounts generate at the same rate** (draft
  recommendation): the loop is the growth engine; a second-class
  generator would stall it. The counter-consideration — a gifted
  account cost no one $5 — is exactly what the credit price is
  for: a gift costs the giver real hours of generous play.
- **Anti-abuse shape:** the credit price of a gift is the wall —
  farming one gift must take longer than the fun of playing
  honestly. Draft guards: generation tied to varied activity (not
  one repeated verb), per-day caps, gifts one-way (an unlock can't
  be un-gifted or traded back into credits), and the phext.tech
  sign-in the shared world already uses as the identity anchor.
  Named plainly: one person with many free alts is the attack;
  the caps and the price are the defense; nothing here needs
  identity checks beyond the account.

### 2.7 THE FORK — can free players ever hold credits? (Will rules)

This is the sharpest open question in the economy. Both ways are
presented plainly:

**Fork A — credits are strictly paid-side.**
Free players never hold credits. Power-ups are visible in free
play — greyed, priced, obviously waiting — and every one of them
says the same thing: generators earn these. The line is maximally
obvious (the ruling's word), the upsell needs no explaining, and
the economy has exactly one faucet to balance. Cost: the free tier
wears a shop window it can never use until a friend gifts it or
$5 changes hands; daily prizes can never be credits; a free
player's only path to a power-up runs through another person.

**Fork B — free players can hold credits from named faucets.**
The faucets (PROPOSAL set): daily-challenge prize credits (small,
once a day); a generator **handing credits directly** to a free
friend (generosity as an on-ramp — and a taste of §2.6 before the
full gift); token→credit conversion at a steep rate (draft 50:1,
capped weekly). Cost: three faucets to balance instead of one;
conversion invites grinding; the line gets softer — "obvious"
must then be carried by *scale* (free credits arrive in ones,
generator income arrives in streams) rather than by kind.
Benefit: a free player can earn one small power-up by play alone,
which makes the paid advantage tangible instead of theoretical —
they have felt what credits do before they're asked for $5.

The design, as ruled:

- **The boss is a dragon.** It is the daily world boss — one
  dragon, one day, **winner take all**.
- **Finding the dragon is itself hard.** Discovery is part of the
  challenge: the day's hunt begins with the search, not the fight.
- **The kill requires 40 friends in full armor and weapons.** Not
  40 onlookers — 40 equipped fighters.
- **The clock is 2 minutes from engaging.** Once the fight starts,
  the dragon dies inside two minutes or the attempt fails.
- **Engagement is exclusive (RULED, 12:10 CST).** Once a group
  engages the dragon, no further attempts may begin until that
  fight resolves — the kill, or the 2 minutes expiring with the
  item loss. Engagement is a claim on the attempt: no second
  company queues up, probes, or piles in mid-fight. A group must
  therefore arrive with its 40 ready — there is no probing
  attempt; engaging IS the attempt.
- **Failure costs everything equipped.** Every participant loses
  all equipped items — and the dragon **hoards** them. The gear is
  not deleted; it piles up in the hoard. Every piece at stake
  carries one of the five rarity tiers ruled at 15:56 CST (§2.14):
  what a company risks in the fight is read off its colors.
- **Winner take all, daily.** The group that kills the dragon
  takes the hoard — today's stakes plus everything earlier losers
  fed it. A lost kit is recoverable the only way the world allows:
  be on the side that wins a later day.
- **The encounter's heart is yelling (RULED, 2026-11-24
  13:45 CST).** Will, verbatim: "the game mechanic is
  players yelling at the dragon, hoping to unlock the
  mystery." Players shout phrases at the dragon, hoping
  one unlocks the mystery — that is the public loop of
  the encounter: what players visibly do, and what they
  hope for, every time they face it.
- **The dragon taunts its challengers (RULED, 12:19 CST).**
  The dragon has a voice, and it uses it to mock the companies
  that come for it. Its mood follows the moon — some days it is
  looser-tongued than others, some days nearly silent.
- **The daily mechanic is the dragon bragging (RULED,
  2026-11-24 16:01 CST).** Will, verbatim: "the daily
  mechanic is mostly the dragon bragging about its
  conquests." The dragon's daily presence is a braggart's
  chronicle: past victories retold with vanity — the
  companies that came for it and fell, the gear it took
  from them and hoards still. That bragging is the
  substance of the daily mechanic: most of what the dragon
  does on an ordinary day is boast about the days before.
  The design consequence, in one line: the bragging is the
  dragon's voice at daily cadence — the taunts (above)
  live inside it, aimed at today's challengers between
  yesterday's triumphs — and a braggart leaks intelligence:
  what it boasts about taking is a record of what the
  fallen were wearing.
- **The dragon is singular, and he can only post what is
  written on his scales (RULED, 2026-11-24 16:02 CST).**
  Will, verbatim: "the dragon is singular in his bragging,
  he can only post what is written on his scales." There
  is one dragon and one voice, and his bragging (above)
  is constrained by his own hide: every brag is composed
  of scale-phrases — the corpus itself surfacing — so his
  daily posts slowly publish his own hide to the world.
  The design consequence, in one line: the bragging is a
  discovery channel — the world reads the dragon by
  reading his brags, and a token's phrases are lines the
  dragon himself may one day say.
- **No audit of yells, ever — by construction (RULED,
  2026-11-24 16:03 CST).** Will, verbatim: "thus, we
  never have to audit what people yell to him."
  Recognition is closed-world: the only yells the game
  can ever recognize are the dragon's own phrases — what
  is written on his scales. An off-corpus yell is inert:
  the dragon cannot repeat it, reward it, or react to
  it. So the yell channel carries no open content
  surface and nothing to audit — no moderation review of
  player yells, ever, by construction rather than by
  policy.
- **The dragon offers secrets (RULED, 12:21 CST).** The
  dragon does not only fight — it offers secrets, millions of
  them, and taking one is a gamble: half of what it offers
  causes further harm, and half grants assistance. The dragon
  is a dangerous oracle as well as a boss: what it knows can
  help you, and it is exactly as likely to hurt.
- **Dragon puzzling requires millions collaborating (RULED,
  2026-11-24 12:28 CST).** Will, verbatim: "ensure that
  dragon puzzling is something that requires millions of
  humans collaborating to solve." Solving the dragon's
  puzzle/secret layer is a mass-collaboration feat by
  design — on the order of millions of humans. It is not
  solvable by one player, by a small team, or by datamining
  alone. The requirement is grounded in systems already
  ruled, not new machinery: the sheer volume of the secrets
  (millions, §2.8 above); daily play pacing how much any
  one person can encounter; the taunts' moon-phase
  oscillation (§2.8), which makes any single observer's
  signal partial and time-smeared — no one watcher ever
  sees the whole cycle clearly; and the never-obvious cap
  (§2.8), which keeps the information in each fragment
  tiny. No individual accumulates enough, fast enough,
  clearly enough: the picture only exists at population
  scale.
- **Staff are immune, and staff win nothing (RULED, 12:12 CST —
  §2.10).** A phext staff member in a failed fight does not lose
  their equipped items — the dragon does not take staff gear.
  And a staff member in a winning company takes no share of the
  hoard. The two halves are one rule, stated whole in §2.10.
  Whether staff count toward the 40 is open (D26).

How the ruling interlocks with today's earlier rulings (his
design, drawn out — not new invention):

- **Full armor and weapons are power-ups, and every power-up
  needs credits** (§2.5). Fielding 40 fully equipped friends is
  therefore a credit-economy feat before it is a combat feat —
  which makes generator status (§2.2) the engine under the boss:
  the players who generate are the players who can arm a company.
- **40 equipped friends is what the gifting loop produces**
  (§2.6). Credits gift the game; gifted friends become generators;
  generators earn the credits that arm the next friend. The dragon
  is the sink that loop was missing: a daily reason to have spent
  weeks turning friends into equipped generators.
- **The hoard is the item economy's memory.** Nothing equipped is
  ever destroyed by a loss — it waits in the hoard as the next
  day's prize. Losing hurts (your kit is gone from your back) and
  is never final (it can be won back), which is what makes a
  2-minute all-or-nothing window fair to attempt daily.

Open mechanics — PROPOSALS for Will, none settled (decisions
D17–D21 in §6):

- **Who counts as one of the 40 "friends"**: accounts you gifted
  (§2.6) / any player in your party / any 40 equipped players
  present in the realm when the fight starts? The gifting reading
  is the one the loop implies; the present-in-realm reading is
  the simplest to build and the easiest to stumble into by
  accident.
- **Where the dragon hides**: a different realm each day (the
  eight shut realms give the hunt somewhere to go) / deep in the
  day's world, unmarked until found / found only by following
  signs (hoard-scatter, frightened pockets)? And does finding it
  announce itself to the whole world, or only to the finders —
  the finders' head start is a real strategic asset.
- **What starts the 2-minute clock**: the first strike / the
  moment the 40th equipped friend enters the fight / the dragon
  noticing the company? First strike is the cleanest to explain;
  the 40th arrival is the one that matches "requires 40 friends."
- **Splitting the hoard among 40 winners**: even split / split
  by what each fighter did in the 2 minutes / the company leader
  divides it? Even is simplest and matches "winner take all" as
  a company prize; by-contribution rewards the geared.
- **Can free-tier players stand in the 40?** Armor and weapons
  need credits, so this rides directly on the fork (D12): under
  Fork A a free friend can only fight if a generator arms them
  (gifted credits or gifted gear — neither exists as a mechanic
  yet); under Fork B a free player might earn their own kit
  slowly. Either way the dragon makes the fork concrete: it is
  the first design element where the line decides who can play.
- **After a fight resolves**: the during-fight lockout is
  settled (RULED above — no new attempt until the fight
  resolves). What the ruling does not address, left open: may
  the same group re-engage after a resolution — re-arm and try
  again the same day, or is one engagement the day's whole
  attempt? Re-engagement makes the hoard grow faster; a single
  daily attempt makes the 2 minutes mean the most.
- **The secrets' shape (D40)**: how are secrets offered —
  inside the taunts, on engagement, to those the dragon has
  killed, on the shared-world board? What do harm and
  assistance mean mechanically — candidate shapes drawn from
  systems the plan already has: items lost or granted,
  credits or tokens moved, the dial shifted, a realm shown
  or shut. Is the half-and-half split exact by count, or by
  effect? Can an offered secret be refused? And is each
  secret unique to the player who takes it, or shared
  worldwide — the same secrets waiting for everyone?
- **Calibrating the collaboration requirement (D41)**: the
  ruling above fixes the scale; how the scale is achieved
  is proposal, not settled. Draft directions: fragments
  distributed so different players encounter different
  secrets (no two players hold the same subset); the
  assembled picture existing only when fragments are pooled

  collaboration surface where the pooling happens;
  encounter rates sized so total coverage of the secret
  space needs on the order of millions of participants;
  and no complete catalog obtainable from the client or
  the game files, so the pooling has to happen in the
  world, between people, not in a datamine.

#### The day of the dragon (DRAFT — the daily loop, end to end)

One player's day, from first light to close, under the rulings
above. This subsection adds no new ruling; it lays the ruled
pieces in the order a player lives them, and names the open
decisions where the day touches them.

- **Day-start — the dragon hides.** The day opens and the
  dragon is somewhere in the world, hidden. Where it hides,
  and how the hunt reads its hiding place, is the discovery
  shape in force (D18 — three shapes are compared in the next
  subsection; none is settled). What is settled: finding it
  is hard, and the hunt, not the fight, is where the day
  begins.
- **The dragon brags.** The day's other opening fact is
  sound: wherever it hides, the dragon spends the day
  bragging about its conquests (RULED, 16:01 CST) — the
  chronicle of the companies it has beaten and the gear it
  took from them, retold with vanity. The bragging is the
  daily mechanic's substance, and it is intelligence as
  well as color: what a braggart boasts about taking is a
  record of what the fallen were wearing.
- **The hunt.** Players search. Search is ordinary play with
  a purpose: crossing realms, reading the world, following
  whatever the discovery shape gives the fox to follow. The
  hunt costs the day nothing but time, and it can be done
  by anyone — the search is the day's open door.
- **Discovery — and the finders' position.** Someone finds
  the dragon. The finders hold a real advantage: they know
  where it is before anyone else does. Whether finding is
  announced to the whole world at once, or only to the
  finders — who then choose when and whom to tell — is open
  (D18). Either way, the finders' head start is the hunt's
  prize: time to muster while others are still searching.
- **The muster.** A company forms: 40 friends, in full armor
  and weapons. This is where the credit economy feeds the
  day. A full kit is 2,850 credits a fighter (§2.14, Band 3:
  six armor pieces totaling 2,250, plus the War Lance at
  600), so a full company stands in 114,000 credits of gear
  — gear that generation (§2.2), gifting (§2.6), and the
  solver (§2.13) spent weeks putting on backs. The muster is
  the gifting loop made visible: friends a player helped
  arm, standing in one place, ready. A company that is short
  — of friends, or of gear — may ask for help (§2.11), up to
  minimum viable kill and no further.
- **The engagement claim.** The company engages. Engagement
  is exclusive (RULED): from that moment, no other company
  may begin an attempt, anywhere in the world, until this
  fight resolves. Engaging is the attempt — there is no
  probe, no test pull, no second company waiting in the
  wings (the state machine below states this formally).
- **The 2 minutes.** The clock runs from engaging (what
  exactly starts it is a named parameter in the state
  machine below — D18). The company's public work in those
  2 minutes is the encounter's heart as ruled: players yell
  at the dragon, hoping to unlock the mystery; the dragon
  taunts back, its mood following the moon, and it may offer
  secrets — half harm, half assistance (the secrets'
  mechanics are open at D40, and dragon puzzling at the
  ruled scale of millions collaborating is open at D41).
  Two minutes is all of it: kill the dragon inside the
  window, or the window closes on the company.
- **Resolution — the kill.** The dragon dies inside the
  2 minutes. The killing company takes the hoard, whole:
  winner take all. The hoard is everything the dragon has
  taken — today's stakes and every earlier loss still
  piled in it. How the company divides the hoard among its
  40 is open (D19).
- **Resolution — the failure.** The 2 minutes expire and
  the dragon lives. Every participant loses all equipped
  items, and the dragon hoards them: the kits come off the
  company's backs and into the pile the next winners will
  take. Nothing is destroyed; everything is waiting to be
  won back. (Staff stand under §2.10: the dragon does not
  take staff gear, and staff take no share of a win.)
- **The lockout lifts.** Resolution — kill or failure — is
  the only thing that ends the engagement claim. When the
  fight resolves, the world is free of the claim again.
  Whether anyone may engage again the same day after a
  resolution is open (D21 — stated as a variant switch in
  the state machine below).
- **Day's close — the hoard carries over.** The day ends.
  If the dragon was killed, its hoard went to the killers
  and a new day begins with whatever the next dragon
  gathers. If the dragon was never killed — never found,
  or found and unbeaten — the hoard carries across days,
  growing with every failed company, until the day a
  company takes it whole.

How this loop sits beside the solo daily pocket (§2.4) —
without settling D22: the pocket is one player's daily
challenge; the dragon is the world's daily challenge, and
the two run on different clocks, different company, and
different stakes. The loop above is written to work under
either D22 ruling. If the dragon **stands beside** the
pocket, a player's day holds both: the pocket alone, at
their own hour, and the hunt, the muster, and the
2 minutes with their company. If the dragon **takes over
the pocket's prize shape**, the pocket stays the solo
daily a player can always reach, and the dragon becomes
the day's great prize at the end of the hunt — the loop's
steps do not change; only what the day's prize is called,
and where it is won, moves. Nothing in the hunt, the
muster, the claim, or the resolution depends on which way
D22 falls.

#### Finding the dragon: three shapes compared (PREPARED FOR WILL — D18)

**Shape 1 — Sign in the world.** The dragon leaves readable

it freshens and fades with the day. One rung reads it
best: Hoard-Scent (§2.14, Band 3, 250 credits) — the
player who can smell a dragon's leavings, days old, reads
sign others walk past. Sign gives the hunt a skill: the
more of the world a company covers, and the better its
readers, the sooner the dragon is found.

**Shape 2 — The dial as dowsing rod.** The gravity dial
trembles and pulls as the fox nears the dragon: a weak
shiver at a distance, a hard pull close in, so the hunt
becomes an instrument reading — walk, watch the dial,
turn toward the pull. The dial's stops are Float ¼×,
Light ½×, Standard 1×, Heavy 1½×, Crushing 2×
(GRAVITY-DIAL.md). The tier interplay must be stated
plainly: the dial's full range is a paid candidate in §3
(item 3) — under that draft, free play keeps the flip and
Standard, and the dial sits visible, stopping at the flip
points. So under Shape 2, what free players get is the
dial at Standard only: at most a plain yes-or-no tremble
at the one stop they hold, with the finer reading — the
pull that strengthens stop by stop — riding on the paid
range, if Will ships §3 item 3 as drafted. Whether the
dowsing reading itself is part of the free Standard stop
or part of the paid sweep is a fork inside this shape
that D18 and D1 would settle together.

**Shape 3 — Generators sense it first.** The spew stills
and drifts near the dragon: where the dragon lies, the
flow of tokens around a generator thins, bends, or stops
for a breath. Generators — paid players, and friends
gifted the game who generate in their turn (§2.6) — feel
the dragon's nearness in the very thing that makes them
generators, before any sign is read or any dial trembles.
Free players get no such sense: they hunt by following
generators, by word of mouth, or by luck.

**RECOMMENDATION (marked, for Will — D18): Shape 1, sign
in the world.** It is the only shape where the hunt — the
day's opening act, and the part every player can reach —
is open to free and paid alike on the same terms, with
the paid edge (Hoard-Scent) widening a skill everyone
already has instead of gating the sense itself. Its
build cost is the highest of the three, and its failure
mode is real; both are the price of a hunt that belongs
to the whole world rather than to one tier or one
instrument.

#### The engagement lockout as a state machine (DRAFT — from the rulings only)

The lockout is ruled (2026-11-24 12:10 CST); this
subsection only writes it as states and transitions, and
parameterizes the two points the rulings leave open.

**The lockout guard, stated formally:** at any moment,
worldwide, there is **at most one** company in the
Engaged state. Engagement begins the attempt. No
transition into Engaged exists from Engaged. Resolution
— the kill, or the 2 minutes expiring — is the only exit
from Engaged. No state may be skipped, and no second
claim may be taken, queued, or held while one stands.

| State | Entered by | Exits to | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hidden | Day-start: the dragon hides, somewhere in the world, under the discovery shape in force (D18). | Found | The hunt is live. No claim exists; any number of players may search. |
| Found | A finder reaches the dragon (announcement — world-wide or finders-only — is part of D18, unsettled). | Engaged | The finders hold their head start. Found is not a claim: another company that also finds the dragon may still be the one to engage first. |
| Engaged | A company engages — the attempt begins, and the 2-minute clock starts (clock-start parameter below). | Resolved (killed) or Resolved (failed) | The lockout guard binds here: exactly one Engaged at a time, worldwide. No new attempt, no queue, no pile-in, until resolution. |
| Resolved (killed) | The dragon dies inside the 2 minutes. | Closed | The killing company takes the hoard whole (division open, D19). The day's dragon is dead; there is nothing left to engage. |
| Resolved (failed) | The 2 minutes expire with the dragon alive. | Closed, or back to Found (variant switch below — D21) | Every participant loses all equipped items to the hoard (staff excepted, §2.10). The hoard grows by the company's kits. |
| Closed | Resolution with the day spent, or day's end with the dragon unfound or unbeaten. | Hidden (next day-start) | The hoard carries across days until a killing company takes it whole. |

**Parameter — the clock-start trigger (D18, unsettled).**
The state machine runs under either setting; the setting
changes what a company must have ready before it engages.

- *Setting A — first strike:* the 2 minutes begin at the
  engaging company's first blow. A company may engage,
  take its positions, and breathe once before the clock
  runs — but engaging has already claimed the attempt, so
  the lockout binds during that breath, and no other
  company may use the time either.
- *Setting B — the 40th arrival:* the 2 minutes begin
  when the 40th equipped friend enters the fight. The
  clock then measures exactly the ruled thing — 40 friends
  in full armor and weapons, fighting — and a company that
  engages short of 40 has claimed the attempt without
  starting its clock, a stall the design would need a
  guard against (none is ruled; flagged, not invented).

**Variant switch — re-engagement after resolution (D21,
unsettled).** Both settings shown, with what each does to
the hoard and to the day:

- *Setting A — one engagement is the day's whole attempt:*
  Resolved exits only to Closed. A failed company is done
  for the day, and so is everyone — the dragon, beaten or
  unbeaten, is finished at the first resolution of a kill,
  and after a failure the dragon withdraws for the day.
  Consequences: the hoard grows by at most one company's
  kits a day, slowly and surely; the 2 minutes carry the
  whole day's meaning, and the muster — arriving truly
  ready — is the day's real skill.
- *Setting B — re-engagement is allowed after a
  resolution:* Resolved (failed) exits back to Found, and
  a company — the same one re-armed, or another — may
  engage again the same day, under the same lockout guard
  (one Engaged at a time, always). Consequences: the hoard
  can grow by several companies' kits in a single day, so
  winner-take-all prizes swell faster and the dragon's
  day has a rhythm of attempts instead of a single throw;
  the day's meaning shifts from one perfect muster to
  persistence — and a rich company can feed the hoard
  repeatedly chasing the kill, which is either the day's
  drama or its drain, as Will judges it.

A kill resolves the day under both settings: Resolved
(killed) exits to Closed, and the next Hidden is the next
day's.

### 2.11 Help for small groups — ask, and it is given to the measure of your company (RULED)

Will, 2026-11-24 12:14 CST, verbatim: "small groups can ask
for help, it will be provided at the average of their group
capability, up to minimum viable kill"

The design, as ruled:

- **Small groups can ask for help.** Asking is an in-game act —
  a group that knows it is short can request help; nothing
  arrives unasked.
- **Help is provided at the average of the group's
  capability.** The help is measured against the group as it
  stands — all of it, averaged — not against its strongest
  member, and not against the strongest group in the world.
- **Help tops the group up to minimum viable kill, and no
  further.** The ceiling on help is the floor of the kill: it
  makes the dragon killable by this group, barely, and stops
  there. Help never makes the fight comfortable, and never
  carries a group past viability into ease.
- **A kill at that floor is marked.** The help system makes
  MVK kills possible; the special MVK achievement (§2.9) is
  what marks them on the global record — ruled by Will,
  2026-11-24 12:14 CST: "special achievement for MVK".

What the ruling does to the design (drawn only from ruled
material):

- **One champion cannot game it.** Importing a single geared
  hero into a weak group barely moves the group's average —
  and even where the average rises, the viability cap binds:
  help stops at minimum viable kill regardless. There is no
  carry to buy.
- **Raising the whole group raises the help.** Because help
  tracks the average, a company that has armed everyone —
  which is what the gifting loop (§2.6) produces — is met at
  a higher measure than a company with one hero and thirty-nine
  bare backs. Help rewards the broad lift, not the spike.
- **Help and staff may be the same door — QUESTION, not a
  settlement.** Staff already stand in the world immune and
  prizeless, able to stand in a fight and take nothing home
  (§2.10). Whether the help a group asks for comes *through*
  the keepers — or from the guide, or from the world itself —
  is Will's call (D29). The pairing is suggestive; this plan
  does not assume it.

Open — PROPOSALS for Will (§6), none settled:

- **Who or what provides the help (D29):** the keepers (phext
  staff under §2.10's law), the guide, or the world itself
  answering the ask? Each gives the ask a different face — a
  person who shows up, a voice that steadies you, or a change
  in the fight itself.
- **Headcount or capability (D30):** does help substitute for
  missing friends toward the 40 the dragon requires (§2.8),
  or only raise the capability of the friends actually
  present? The first reading softens the 40; the second keeps
  the 40 absolute and lets help speak only to strength.
- **What "capability" is measured in (D31):** the gear worn
  (armor and weapons, §2.5), the credits behind the gear, the
  group's achievements (§2.9) — or some weave of them? The
  measure decides what a group grows to be helped more.
- **How often a group may ask (D32):** once per day, once per
  engagement, or freely? A limited ask is a decision a group
  spends; a free ask is a dial every small group turns.
- **Helped kills and the hoard (D33):** does a kill made with
  help take the hoard (§2.8) on the same winner-take-all
  terms, or does asking change what the winners carry home?

#### The help formula (DRAFT — a faithful formalization of the ruling, PREPARED FOR WILL)

The ruling in symbols. Nothing below adds to the ruling;
where a measure must be chosen to make the ruling
computable, the choice is marked PROPOSAL and the formula
is written so it survives the choice being changed.

- **Per-fighter capability, c_i (PROPOSAL measure).** For
  each fighter in the company present, c_i is the
  equipped-kit fraction: the credit value of what that
  fighter wears and wields, from §2.14 Band 3, divided by
  the full-kit value of 2,850 credits (six armor pieces
  totaling 2,250, plus the War Lance at 600). A fighter in
  a full kit has c_i = 1.0; a fighter in half a kit by
  value has c_i = 0.5; a fighter with nothing has
  c_i = 0. D31 may choose a different measure — gear
  counted another way, the credits behind the gear,
  achievements, or a weave — and the formula below
  survives the swap unchanged: only the numbers fed into
  it change.
- **Group capability, C.** C is the mean of c_i over the
  company present: C = (Σ c_i) / (number of fighters
  present). This is the ruling's "average of their group
  capability," taken over the group as it stands when it
  asks.
- **The MVK floor, M (PROPOSAL draft value).** M is the
  total effective capability a kill minimally requires,
  expressed in full-kit equivalents. **Draft value:
  M = 30** — a kill is minimally viable at the strength
  of 30 full kits, however that strength is assembled.
  The value is a draft for Will to move; the formula's
  shape does not depend on it.
- **Help.** When a company asks, the world adds helper
  strength in units that each fight at exactly C — the
  group's average, the ruling's words, not one point
  stronger. Help adds the smallest number of such units
  that lifts the company's total effective capability to
  M, and no more:

  **n_help = ceil((M − Σ c_i) / C)**, and **n_help = 0
  when Σ c_i ≥ M**.

  The company's total with help is Σ c_i + n_help × C,
  which is at least M and, because the count is the
  smallest whole number that reaches M, less than one
  helper-unit past it. Help stops at the floor of the
  kill, as ruled — never past it. (The edge case the
  formula names honestly: if C = 0 — a company with no
  gear at all — no finite number of units fighting at
  C adds any strength, and help cannot lift the company.
  A group must bring something to be helped; the ruling's
  average gives the world nothing to answer with when the
  average is nothing.)

**Worked example 1 — a full company of 40, undergeared.**
Forty friends present, each at c_i = 0.5 (half a kit by
value, 1,425 credits of gear a fighter). Then Σ c_i = 40 ×
0.5 = **20**, and C = **0.5**. Help owed:
n_help = ceil((30 − 20) / 0.5) = ceil(20) = **20 helper
units**, each fighting at 0.5. Total effective capability:
20 + 20 × 0.5 = **30 = M, exactly**. Headline: a full but
half-geared forty is met by 20 helpers at its own measure,
and stands at the floor — viable, and not one point
comfortable.

**Worked example 2 — a short company of 25, well-geared.**
Twenty-five friends present, each at c_i = 0.75 (2,137.5
credits of gear a fighter by value). Then Σ c_i = 25 ×
0.75 = **18.75**, and C = **0.75**. Help owed:
n_help = ceil((30 − 18.75) / 0.75) = ceil(15) = **15 helper
units**, each fighting at 0.75. Total effective
capability: 18.75 + 15 × 0.75 = **30 = M, exactly**.
Headline: 15 helpers at the company's own measure lift a
strong 25 to the floor. What those 15 helpers mean for
the ruled 40 is exactly D30, and the arithmetic splits
on it:

- *Under the headcount-substitution reading (D30, first
  variant):* the helpers also count toward the 40. The
  company in the fight is 25 friends + 15 helpers = **40
  exactly** — the headcount and the capability floor are
  met by the same 15 units, and the fight may begin.
- *Under the capability-only reading (D30, second
  variant):* the 40 must be real friends, and help speaks
  only to strength. The same 15 helpers lift the company's
  strength to M, but the company still stands at **25 of
  the required 40 friends** — help alone cannot begin
  this fight. The company must muster 15 more real friends
  first (whose gear would then change Σ c_i and C, and the
  help would be figured again on the company as it then
  stands).

**The anti-carry property, in the formula's terms.** One
hero barely moves the help a group is owed, for two
reasons that are both in the symbols. First, a single
c_i = 1.0 among many low c_i raises Σ c_i a little but
moves C — the mean over the whole company — barely at
all, so the helper units the world sends still fight at
the group's low average, and many of them are needed.
Second, the cap binds regardless: help stops at M
whatever Σ c_i and C are, so no arrangement of one strong
fighter and many weak ones can turn help into ease. The
only way to be met at a higher measure is to raise the
whole company's c_i — the broad lift, not the spike.

**Interactions flagged, none settled.** D32 (how often a
group may ask) decides when the formula is figured —
once a day, once an engagement, or at every ask, the
numbers are figured on the company as it stands at that
ask. D33 (helped kills and the hoard) decides what a kill
at M takes home; the formula itself is the same under
either answer. D34 (judging MVK) decides the moment that
counts — if MVK is judged at engagement, Σ c_i and C are
read when the company engages; if across the whole fight,
a company that loses gear or fighters mid-fight could
fall in or out of the mark, and the formula would need
its reading moment named. The special MVK achievement
(§2.9) marks kills this formula makes possible, under
whichever settings Will rules.

Player-facing shape (under the veil, draft wording): *"Too
few of you, or too lightly armed? You can ask. The world
answers at the measure of your company as it stands — and
never past enough. Help will make the kill possible. It will
not make it easy."*

### 2.16 Portals and monsters — geometry, two-way persistence, and carried populations (RULED)

Playtest feedback, Will, 2026-11-24 18:14 CST, verbatim:

- "portals should have geometry, based upon how far apart
  they are in subspace"
- "portals remain open and traversable if the player is
  within range - it's not a 1-way trip"
- "monsters start out native to different regions of
  subspace, but can be carried across by the rifts players
  open"

Three rulings:

1. **Portal geometry encodes subspace distance (RULED).**
   A portal's geometry — its size and shape — is a
   function of how far apart its two ends are in
   subspace. Distance between realms is a physical fact
   of the world, and the portal wears it.
2. **Portals persist in range and run both ways
   (RULED).** A portal remains open and traversable
   while the player is within range. Crossing is not a
   one-way trip: the same portal takes you back.
3. **Monsters are region-native, and rifts are their
   migration vector (RULED).** At world start, each
   monster population is native to its own region of
   subspace. Populations do not stay put by nature —
   they move when players open rifts and carry them
   across. A monster out of its region is there because
   a player brought it.

Derived from the rulings (draft consequences of the
rulings as stated — not new rulings):

- **Distance you can read.** If geometry is a function
  of subspace distance, the function runs both ways for
  the player: stand in front of a portal and its shape
  tells you how far it goes, before you step through —
  near realms and far realms announce themselves
  differently. Portal-reading becomes an exploration
  skill of the early game, and §2.4's draft sink — a
  long rift ride costs tokens — gains a visible basis:
  the price is legible in the portal's shape before it
  is paid. Which geometric properties carry the
  distance, and whether the mapping is stepped or
  continuous, is D53.
- **Range is the portal's leash.** Persistence is
  conditional on the player being within range — so
  "range" plausibly measures the player's in-world
  distance from the portal (most simply, from the end
  the player is standing at), and the portal folds
  when its player walks away beyond it. A portal is a
  held-open door, not a permanent fixture: travel is
  planned around who holds which door open. Whether
  range is measured from the opener or from any player
  in range, and what happens mid-crossing when range
  lapses, is D54.
- **Carrying is a player act with ecological weight.**
  Because the vector is "the rifts players open," no
  monster crosses on its own schedule at world start —
  a cross-region monster is a player-caused change to
  a region's ecology, traceable to someone's rift. It
  is an achievement-shaped act (§2.9's major-action
  list gains candidates: first carrying, first
  establishment) and a shared-world one: one player's
  carrying changes the region for every player who
  visits it after. How carrying is done — and whether
  a monster can also follow or wander through a
  held-open rift uninvited — is D55; whether carried
  populations establish, breed, and persist is D56.

**Build note (2026-11-24, against the staged portals +
palettes build, sha256 `e76963d1…`):** the build's round
trip already works — its own e2e crosses to Ashfall and
home (84/84, PLAYTEST.md) — but its mechanism differs
from ruling 2 in three ways, and from ruling 1 in one:

- *Persistence:* a build rift lives on a fixed
  ~25-second timer and folds when the timer lapses,
  crossed or not ("folds shut, uncrossed, after ~25
  seconds," game3.js); nothing in the build measures
  player range. Ruling 2 makes persistence
  range-conditional instead.
- *Two-way:* crossing consumes the build's rift
  (`closeRift()` runs on the cross); the way home is a
  *new* rift opened by replaying the tune from the far
  side — the guide's crossing lesson teaches exactly
  this. The trip is round, but it is two one-way doors,
  not one door traversed twice. Ruling 2 describes one
  door, both ways, while it stands.
- *Singularity:* only one rift can stand at a time in
  the build (openRift folds any other); the rulings do
  not speak to a cap.
- *Geometry:* the build's ring is one fixed form for
  every destination; the ring wears the destination
  realm's palette accent, so a portal today announces
  *where* it goes, not *how far*. Ruling 1 adds the
